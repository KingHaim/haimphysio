When I was on the WTA tour working with players like Karolina Pliskova, we didn't just focus on muscles and joints—we treated the athlete as a complete system. One of the most overlooked yet critical aspects of high-performance recovery is gut health, specifically how the stomach's anatomy functions under extreme physical stress. In professional tennis, we can't afford downtime from something as debilitating as gastritis or reflux, which can derail training and competition faster than any muscle strain.

**The High-Performance Problem: When Your Stomach Becomes a Weak Link**

For elite athletes, the stomach isn't just for digestion—it's a performance organ. Intense training, travel, and competition schedules put immense stress on the gastrointestinal system. The antrum (the lower part of the stomach) and pylorus (the valve controlling food passage to the small intestine) must work flawlessly to prevent duodenogastric reflux—where bile and digestive enzymes flow backward into the stomach. When these structures are compromised, often post-surgery or due to nerve damage, it leads to inflammation (gastritis), pain, nausea, and nutrient malabsorption. This isn't just discomfort; it's a direct hit to recovery, energy levels, and peak performance.

**The Tour Insight: How Pros Manage Gut Integrity**

This is the exact protocol we used with top players: we treated gut health with the same precision as a shoulder rehab. We understood that an innervated (properly nerve-supplied) and intact antrum and pylorus are non-negotiable for preventing reflux. On tour, we collaborated with sports gastroenterologists to ensure any abdominal issues—whether from injury, surgery, or stress—were addressed with a focus on preserving neural function and anatomical integrity. We'd monitor symptoms like post-meal bloating or burning pain as red flags, because in our world, even minor gut dysfunction can mean losing a critical match edge.

**3 Actionable Steps to Optimize Your Gut for Performance**

1. **Assess and Protect Neural Pathways**: Work with a sports medicine specialist to evaluate any abdominal history, especially surgeries. Ensure your care team prioritizes preserving the nerves around the antrum and pylorus during procedures—this is key to maintaining proper valve function and preventing reflux. Regular check-ins can catch early signs of nerve impairment.

2. **Implement a Pro-Level Nutrition Strategy**: Adopt a diet that supports stomach integrity. Focus on easily digestible, anti-inflammatory foods—think lean proteins, complex carbs, and healthy fats. Avoid triggers like excessive caffeine or spicy foods before training. Time your meals to allow proper gastric emptying; we used precise windows (e.g., 2-3 hours pre-match) to optimize digestion and minimize reflux risk.

3. **Monitor and Adapt with Precision**: Track gut symptoms as rigorously as you do workout metrics. Use a journal to note any reflux, pain, or bloating related to training intensity or travel. If symptoms arise, adjust immediately—reduce load, modify diet, or consult a specialist. On tour, we never ignored gut signals; they're early warnings of bigger performance drops.

In elite sports, every system must fire perfectly. By prioritizing the health of your antrum and pylorus, you're not just avoiding gastritis—you're safeguarding your energy, recovery, and competitive edge. Treat your gut like the performance asset it is, and you'll stay ahead of the game.
